Feature 1 · Plan Farm

From a property ID to a first-year farm plan

1

Find the land by PID (the property ID on the tax notice or deed), address, phone location or photo GPS. The boundary loads from the NS property registry.

2

The agent researches it: frost dates, soil, slope, satellite greenness, land use and forecast.

3

Sam gets a plan: crops, first steps, a 12-month calendar, income, and which programs to contact.

Sam’s land: 156 frost-free days · acidic sandy loam, pH 5.4 · 85% already open farmland → potatoes, strawberries, sweet corn

Screens from the live prototype, using Sam’s land (PID 25158932)

Venkateshwaran: Sam types the PID from the tax notice and the boundary loads from the Nova Scotia property registry. The agent pulls frost, soil, slope, satellite and land-use data. For Sam’s land it found 156 frost-free days and acidic sandy loam, and picked potatoes, strawberries and sweet corn. Sam also gets first steps, a 12-month calendar and who to contact: the Farm Loan Board, Perennia and the Federation of Agriculture.
